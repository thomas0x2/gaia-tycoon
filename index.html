Meridian · population 14 million · year 2061

Let nature take the city back.

The city starts dead: no wild plants, no animals, no seeds coming in. Every species gets its first foothold from you, by hand and for a price.

After that, nature spreads by its own rules, slowly and steadily, and keeps growing while you are away.

First steps

Clear the ground

  • Start by hand. You have one residential block in the middle of the city. Hit buildings to bring them down (a house takes 3 hits) and break up the paving.
  • Sow pioneer weeds on the bare soil. They are the first wild plants, and they start your seed income.
  • Seeds come from wild plants. They earn seeds by themselves, and grown plants now and then hold up a ripe seed: click it to gather a handful more. Every plant earns its own amount, and many do more: reeds clean the soil, gorse feeds it, scrub shelters seedlings.
How nature spreads

Give it a foothold

  • Succession carries on by itself once a plant is researched: weeds, then grassland, scrub and woodland. Each plant spreads fastest next to its own kind. Once plenty grow in the city, their seed rains in everywhere: by wind, or carried by birds, jays, grazers and water, so bring back the animals that carry it.
  • Plants grow up. A grown tile slowly matures through the levels of its plant (a young meadow into a flowering, then a herb-rich, then an ancient meadow), as far as its ground allows: soil, water, light, and the bees and partner animals nearby. Each level earns more seeds. No research needed.
  • Sowing by hand plants a stage where you want it, but it is dear, and every tile you sow raises the price of the next.
  • Release animals by hand. Bring two of a kind where they have habitat and they breed slowly, in spring and summer. Each animal adds something: pollinating, carrying seed, browsing, hunting. Some, like wolves and deer, only settle where the city has gone quiet.
  • Roads are walls. Plants and walking animals only spread through connected land.
Helping it along

Clean up and grow

  • Read the ground. Every tile has its water, light, soil richness, grazing, disturbance, human pressure and toxicity, and every species changes them around it. The Lens shows each one across the map.
  • Grow the Wood Wide Web. Spend seeds to teach the land new plants and animals, and strengthen the forces of nature.
  • Life needs life. New species need enough biodiversity first, and most animals need their food already living in the city.
  • Let nature take over. Roots crack paving and ivy topples buildings next to wild land.
The long game

Discover and expand

  • Find the hidden ecosystems. The right mix of plants and animals in one block starts something new.
  • Some structures are worth keeping. With Reclamation, a motorway flyover becomes a green bridge for walking animals.
  • Grow the map. The city opens ring by ring around your first block. Rewild all 64 blocks, then carry your mycelium on to the next city.
Research

Wood Wide Web

Under every wood runs a web of fungal threads linking the roots. Grow it with seeds. New plants and animals also need the city to be alive enough to hold them: each asks for a level of biodiversity (BIO). Only what adds something new to the game is here: each stage of succession, each animal and each force of nature. Richer plants need no research: grown tiles level up on their own. Each plant or animal you grow makes the rest 12% dearer, and species that need a richer city cost far more. Golden nodes are secrets. Read each tree from the top down; click a node to see what it adds.
